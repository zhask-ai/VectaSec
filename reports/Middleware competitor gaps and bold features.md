# Turn VectaSec From Checklist Into Proof

**As of 23 November 2026.** VectaSec feels small because it presents itself as a list of misconfigurations, and lists are what every rival already sells. The research points to a different product shape: **joins and proofs that no single system can produce by itself**. Examples are "what changed and when" for tools that hide their audit logs behind an enterprise plan, "this finding is really exploitable, here is the transcript", "this tool was breached today, rotate these 9 credentials in this order", and "this person left, here is everything they built". The white space is real but provisional: no company was found that sells security posture for the whole integration layer, and free tools cover it thinly (Prowler has 32 checks for all AWS messaging services together, and zero for Azure Service Bus, Event Hubs or Google Pub/Sub). However, the researchers did not read the rule catalogues of Wiz, Orca, the cloud providers' own tools, or the SaaS security (SSPM) vendors, so every "nobody does this" below means "not found in what was checked". One uncomfortable finding matters for positioning: **no verified user review says "my security tool cannot see my brokers or gateways"**. Buyers feel the gap in two other places: when an auditor asks for logs their plan does not include, and when a vendor that holds their tokens is breached. The ten ideas below are ranked on those two pains first. MCP scanning, MCP gateways and anything that sits in the data path should not be built; they are already free or owned by big platforms.

**How to read this note.** Evidence tags are kept simple. **[verified]** means a researcher opened the page and read it. **[vendor claim]** means a company with something to sell said it. **[summary only]** means it was seen only in a search-result summary and the page was not opened; treat it as a lead. **[inference]** means it is reasoning by the researchers or by me, not a quoted fact. Effort uses the scale from the October report: **S** is up to 1 engineer-week, **M** is 1 to 2 weeks, **L** is 3 to 5 weeks, **XL** is more than 5 weeks or needs new infrastructure. All effort figures are my estimates **[inference]**. "What VectaSec already has" comes from the README and the 17 October code audit; the README itself says several of these features have not yet been run against live systems, and there is no customer yet. Table-stakes gaps (SSO, pricing and billing, public API and so on) are in the October report, `reports/Best middleware security tool feature scope.md`, and are not repeated here.

## Ten bold features, ranked by evidence and effort

The ranking multiplies four things: how strong the evidence of pain is, whether anyone was found doing it, how much of it VectaSec already has in code, and how small the effort is. Ideas 1 and 3 have the best evidence. Ideas 2 and 7 give the best live demo. If the goal is only "make it exciting", build 2 first; if the goal is "make someone pay", build 1 and 3 first.

### 1. Time machine: the audit trail the vendor will not sell you

**The pain.** This is the best-evidenced unmet need in the whole research set. A paying n8n Pro customer needed login and workflow-change logs for an internal IT security review, could not get them, and wrote "I might be at risk to suspend in using n8n just because i can't comply to IT security policies". n8n staff replied that this is the Log streaming feature "available in the Enterprise version"; the customer answered that they "can't justify the ROI yet" **[verified]** ([n8n community](https://community.n8n.io/t/pro-version-with-user-activity-logs-urgent/135551)). The same pattern exists elsewhere. Confluent Cloud Basic clusters do not include audit logs and other tiers keep them for seven days **[summary only, official docs]** ([Confluent docs](https://docs.confluent.io/cloud/current/monitoring/audit-logging/cloud-audit-log-concepts.html)). Kong ended its free mode at version 3.10 and a user noted "the lack of response from the Kong maintainers" **[verified]** ([Kong Nation](https://discuss.konghq.com/t/is-opensource-support-for-kong-ceased/13529)).

**Who does it today.** Nobody was found. No third party rebuilds change history for the lower tiers of integration tools, and the frontier research found no 2026 launch of configuration "time travel" from any security vendor. This rests on searches, not on reading every roadmap.

**What VectaSec already has.** Scheduled scans, an append-only hash-chained evidence ledger, scan history per connection, and a weekly digest that already calculates what is new. The data is there; it is not yet shown as history. The build is a per-resource diff (workflows, ACLs, routes, plugins, connections, credentials), a date picker for "posture as of", a "when did this exposure open" line on each finding, and one exportable "access and change report" for an IT reviewer.

**Effort and honest limits.** S–M. A scanner that polls can show what changed and between which two scans. It can show who made the change only where the system's API exposes an author **[inference]**. It is not a full audit log, and the product must say so. Whether auditors accept it has not been tested; the notes found no evidence either way.

### 2. Proof mode: stop saying "misconfigured", start saying "proven"

**The pain.** Noise is the third most repeated complaint in the review data. A Wiz user wrote "there were too many alerts" **[verified]** ([PeerSpot](https://www.peerspot.com/questions/what-advice-do-you-have-for-others-considering-wiz)), and a Prisma Cloud engineer asked for a way to prove that a flagged resource is really exploitable **[verified, one reviewer]** ([PeerSpot](https://www.peerspot.com/products/prisma-cloud-by-palo-alto-networks-pros-and-cons)). The real-world numbers show that the simplest findings are the ones that hurt. Wiz scanned 3,074 internet-facing LiteLLM gateways and found **294 (9.6%) accepted the default master key** `sk-1234` **[verified]** ([Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/off-guard-breaking-litellm-from-authentication-bypass-to-cloud-compromise)). Censys found 3,979 NATS servers that need no login, 1,230 of them sending live events, and more than 480,000 MQTT hosts that accept anonymous connections; it says the owners "may not know their services are reachable" **[verified]** ([Censys](https://censys.com/blog/unauth-mqueue-problem)).

**Who does it today.** "Exploitable, not just vulnerable" is the best-funded security message of 2026. Horizon3 raised $250M at a valuation above $2B **[summary only]** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/horizon3-hits-2-billion-valuation-with-250m-series-e-as-ai-threats-escalate/)). Wiz announced a system where every finding must be validated with a working exploit, not yet for sale **[verified, vendor claim]** ([Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/wiz-at-black-hat-2026)). Prowler added a verdict (exploitable, likely, conditional, not exploitable) to attack paths **[summary only]** ([Prowler changelog](https://docs.prowler.com/changelog)). No vendor was found doing this against Kafka, RabbitMQ, NATS or automation tools. The researchers warn that "absence of search results is not proof".

**What VectaSec already has.** About 440 deterministic detectors, read-only fingerprinting before login, the DNS ownership gate from the free exposure scan, and the evidence ledger. Proof mode needs no AI. For a finding such as "anonymous access enabled", VectaSec performs one bounded action on a system the customer has connected or proved they own: list topics without credentials, call the unauthenticated admin endpoint, try the documented default key, ask an MCP server for its tool list. It stores the transcript and marks the finding "exploitable: proven at 14:02". The existing Verify button then becomes the second half of the story: proven open, fixed, proven closed.

**Effort and honest limits.** M for the first five or six probes, more for each new system. The rules must be strict: read-only, one action, never read message contents, only on owned or connected targets. Trust in AI-only pentesting fell during 2026 (one survey reports the share of firms relying only on AI for testing dropped from 29% to 9%) **[summary only; the source sells human-led testing]** ([Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/cybersecurity-operations/ai-decline-confidence-autonomous-penetration-testing)). A deterministic proof avoids that problem, which is why it suits a small team.

### 3. Breach-day mode: the day it is in the news, the product already knows

**The pain.** This idea has the strongest incident record. Attackers exploited a critical Langflow bug about 20 hours after it was disclosed **[secondary coverage]** ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4151203/attackers-exploit-critical-langflow-rce-within-hours-as-cisa-sounds-alarm.html)). About a month after the n8n "Ni8mare" flaw, more than 24,700 instances were still unpatched and exposed **[secondary coverage]** ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/12/cisa_n8n_rce/)). When the agent-integration platform Composio was breached in May 2026, 5,241 API keys were "potentially exposed"; customers were told to rotate keys by a deadline, and Composio could not revoke a share of connections ("under 5%") for them **[verified]** ([Composio](https://composio.dev/blog/composio-may-2026-security-incident)). After the Salesloft Drift breach, Google told victims to hunt by hand: search Salesforce objects for strings like `AKIA` and `password`, and ask Salesforce support for the attacker's queries **[verified]** ([Google Threat Intelligence](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/data-theft-salesforce-instances-via-salesloft-drift)). A weekly scan is too slow for this.

**Who does it today.** Qualys announced matching new advisories against stored inventory without a new scan, for general IT assets **[trade press]** ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/black-hat-usa-2026-summary-of-vendor-announcements-part-2/)). Nothing was found that does it for brokers, gateways and automation tools and then adds "and here is what that system holds".

**What VectaSec already has.** A running-version inventory, OSV and NVD feeds, end-of-life rules, the external exposure scan, the access register with the "held by an automation" flag, and Slack, Teams and email routing.

**Effort, in three stages.** Stage one (S): add the CISA known-exploited list as a feed and send an alert the moment a version you run appears in it: "You run n8n 1.x. It entered the exploited list today. It is reachable from the internet. It holds 14 credentials. Rotate these first." Stage two (M): a vendor kill list for systems VectaSec already connects to, so that "Zapier / n8n / this MCP server is compromised" produces an ordered list of connections, keys and webhooks to revoke. Stage three (L–XL, do not start yet): a register of third-party OAuth grants in Salesforce, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and GitHub. That needs new connectors outside middleware, and it is the home ground of SSPM vendors (AppOmni, Obsidian, Reco, Grip, Wing, Valence) whose coverage the researchers did not read. Check them before spending a week on it.

### 4. Leaver report: one person's automations on one page

**The pain.** OWASP's non-human identity list ranks improper offboarding first **[summary only]** ([OWASP](https://owasp.org/projects/non-human-identities-top-10)). In Zapier, the feature for moving business-critical Zaps from a departing person is limited to Team owners and Company super admins or owners, and a third-party guide notes the account itself belongs to the individual **[summary only]** ([Zapier help](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/16534140057485-Safeguard-business-critical-Zap-workflows-when-a-team-member-transitions)). The usual advice is a manual checklist: for each automation, what exists, who owns it now, which secrets it uses **[third party]** ([PDQ](https://www.pdq.com/blog/it-automation-handover-checklist/)). In n8n, anyone who can edit a shared workflow can use every credential in it, and the community's tool for mapping workflows to credentials is a template that writes to a Google Sheet **[summary only]** ([n8n template](https://n8n.io/workflows/14423-audit-workflow-credential-usage-to-google-sheets-using-google-drive-and-sqlite3/)).

**Who does it today.** Nobody found for n8n, Zapier or Make. Zenity's documentation lists Workato but not n8n, Zapier, Make, Boomi or Airflow **[verified from the docs navigation]** ([Zenity docs](https://docs.zenity.io/)). SSPM vendors' app lists were not read.

**What VectaSec already has.** The access register already has an owner column and connectors for n8n and Zapier. The feature is a filter and a report: pick a person, see every workflow, connection, key and webhook tied to them, with last use and what depends on each.

**Effort and honest limits.** S–M. The Zapier connector has not yet run against a real account, and the result is only as good as the owner data each tool exposes. No first-hand operator quotes were found for this pain; the evidence is the existence of the manual checklists.

### 5. "What breaks if I fix this" preview and rotation runbook

**The pain.** Small teams do not fix things because they fear an outage. The verified part of the evidence is about ownership: a site reliability engineer at a financial services firm with more than 10,000 staff wrote "I have had hard times trying to find secrets that different teams own" **[verified]** ([PeerSpot](https://www.peerspot.com/products/hashicorp-vault-pros-and-cons)). The claim that rotation breaks production because nobody knows the consumers is widely repeated, but mostly by an industry group and vendors, not by users in reviews **[summary only]** ([NHI Mgmt Group](https://nhimg.org/faq/what-breaks-when-secret-rotation-is-not-tied-to-ownership-review/)). Amazon's documented way to rotate a Kafka password is manual: create a new user, copy the ACLs, wait, verify, move the clients **[summary only]** ([AWS MSK docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/msk/latest/developerguide/msk-password-rotation.html)).

**Who does it today.** Microsoft's Agent 365 shows which agents depend on an MCP server before an admin blocks it **[verified, vendor page]** ([Microsoft](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/agent-365-blog/whats-new-in-microsoft-agent-365-ignite-2026/4571753)). Nothing similar was found for broker users, gateway keys or automation connections.

**What VectaSec already has.** The graph and its blast-radius endpoint, live connection evidence from brokers, last-seen data, and Guided fix plus Verify. The build is to run the graph "as if" the fix were applied and show two lists before the user acts: attack paths that close, and consumers and workflows that would lose access. For credentials, generate a runbook (create new, move these four consumers, verify, delete old).

**Effort and honest limits.** M. The preview can only list consumers VectaSec has observed. It must say "consumers seen in the last N days", never "all consumers". Pitch it on the ownership quote, which is verified, and not on outage stories, which are not.

### 6. Least-privilege recommender with a dry run

**The pain.** Kafka ACLs record no owner, purpose or usage; wildcard grants quietly widen as topics are added; unused rights are never removed. This is described consistently, but mainly by vendors who sell a fix **[vendor claim]** ([Conduktor](https://www.conduktor.io/blog/kafka-security), [AutoMQ](https://www.automq.com/blog/kafka-user-access-control-roles-acls-and-operational-boundaries)). No open-source tool was found that detects unused or wildcard Kafka ACLs **[summary only]**.

**Who does it today.** Conduktor launched free agents on 4 November 2026 that flag over-broad access and suggest "a tighter grant", for Kafka only **[verified]** ([Conduktor](https://www.conduktor.io/blog/conduktor-agents)). This is new since the October report and weakens the Kafka part of that report's "nobody ships this" claim. Nobody was found doing it across RabbitMQ, NATS, Redis and gateways together.

**What VectaSec already has.** ACL and permission rules for each broker, last-used data, and runtime evidence. The feature proposes the narrowest grant that still covers observed use, and shows a dry-run diff of who would lose what.

**Effort and honest limits.** M. The quality depends on how much real usage each broker exposes; producer activity is harder to observe than consumer groups **[inference]**. Sell it as cross-broker, because Kafka-only is now free from Conduktor.

### 7. Canaries from findings

**The pain.** In the Klue incident the attacker used a real integration account, ran an automated query loop for about 24 hours, and made nearly 1,000 queries in 15 minutes in one environment; the responders say integration identities "are typically monitored far less closely than employee accounts" **[verified]** ([ReliaQuest](https://reliaquest.com/blog/threat-spotlight-integration-abused-in-crm-data-theft)). A team with no security staff cannot watch logs, but it can receive one alert when a fake credential is used.

**Who does it today.** Horizon3 places decoys where its pentest found weak points **[summary only]** ([Horizon3](https://horizon3.ai/nodezero/nodezero-tripwires/)); Acalvio announced honeytokens and decoy tools for AI agents **[trade press]** ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/black-hat-usa-2026-summary-of-vendor-announcements-part-1/)). The researchers did not search specifically for canary tools for brokers or automation platforms, so this is the least-tested white-space claim on the list.

**What VectaSec already has.** Findings that say where an attacker would go first, and alert routing. It does not yet write anything into customer systems.

**Effort and honest limits.** M–L. This would be VectaSec's first feature that creates objects in a customer system. The safest first version is a decoy credential or webhook that points at a VectaSec-hosted endpoint, so any use is seen without reading the customer's logs **[inference]**.

### 8. Publish the missing benchmark

**The pain.** The category has no name. Two extended searches found no company using "middleware security", "integration security posture" or similar terms, no 2026 startup aimed at it, and no analyst category **[summary only]**. That means no competitor, but also no budget line and no checklist for buyers to compare against. The CIS catalogue has no benchmark for Kafka, RabbitMQ, Redis, NATS, Kong, Istio or Airflow **[verified; the page was cut short when read]** ([CIS](https://www.cisecurity.org/cis-benchmarks)). CIS published an MCP Server Benchmark on 16 September 2026, which shows a new benchmark can define a field quickly ([CIS](https://www.cisecurity.org/about-us/media/press-release/cis-releases-cis-mcp-server-benchmark-v1-0-0)).

**Who does it today.** Nobody found. US government hardening guides (DISA STIGs) and vendor guides were not checked.

**What VectaSec already has.** About 440 rules, a per-connector coverage matrix, and the roughly 210 checks listed in the October report's appendix. Publishing the control list (not the detection code) as an open "Integration Layer Benchmark" gives auditors something to point at and makes VectaSec the reference tool for it.

**Effort and honest limits.** S–M of writing. It is a market move, not a product feature. It hands the list of checks to rivals; the judgement here is that the list is easy to copy anyway and the hard part is the per-system access and the joins **[inference]**.

### 9. Agent reach map

**The pain.** Agent security is the most crowded category of 2026, yet governance lags: one survey of 839 IT decision makers found only 18% have standardised agent governance **[trade press]** ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/?p=98114)). Internet-exposed MCP servers grew from 492 to 1,467 in under a year **[vendor claim]** ([Trend Micro](https://www.trendmicro.com/vinfo/tr/security/news/vulnerabilities-and-exploits/update-on-exposed-mcp-servers-the-threat-widens-to-the-cloud)).

**Who does it today.** An index of 17 MCP tools shows scanners that read tool descriptions and gateways that read traffic **[third party]** ([Agent Security Index](https://agentsecurityindex.com/category/mcp-security/)). None of the material read joins an MCP server's own credentials to the brokers, gateways and workflows those credentials can reach. Confidence is medium; Cloudflare, Operant, Noma, Pillar and Prompt Security were not read.

**What VectaSec already has.** An MCP connector and the access register. The narrow version (M) shows, for each MCP server already connected, which credentials it holds and what they reach in the other connected systems. The wide version (L) also pulls agent lists from Microsoft Entra Agent ID or Okta, which needs new connectors.

### 10. Toxic flows and a streaming data map

**The pain.** About three quarters of teams say they have a full API inventory, but fewer than one in four know which APIs touch sensitive data **[vendor survey]** ([Akamai](https://www.akamai.com/blog/security/2026/apr/api-weak-spot-study-ai-compounding-security-pressures)). The same question for topics, queues and workflows has no good answer today.

**Who does it today.** BigID advertises Kafka topic scanning **[summary only, vendor claim]** ([BigID](https://bigid.com/data-coverage/kafka/)). Snyk's free scanner flags "toxic flows", meaning dangerous tool combinations, for agent tools only **[summary only]** ([Snyk agent-scan](https://github.com/snyk/agent-scan)). Confluent and Kong shipped in-stream redaction and field encryption in 2026, but as enforcement in the data path, not as a report of where protection is missing. Whether Cyera, Sentra or Varonis scan streams is unconfirmed.

**What VectaSec already has.** A graph, and data classification that the October audit called thin because it works from names only. This is why the effort is L: classification must first improve (schema fields before names) and only then can the product compute "reads sensitive data and writes to an external place" across topics, workflows and MCP tools.

### Smaller moves that support the ten

Several ideas are packaging more than engineering, and they answer the most repeated complaints. Show three findings by default, never a count as the headline, each with a plain sentence about the consequence; a founder who sells to small teams put it as "What a small team actually needs isn't more findings" **[verified, vendor claim]** ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/wael_matoussi/the-problem-with-security-scanners-isnt-the-scanning-5cgl)). Add a verdict per finding (fix or accept, with confidence), which is the language Wiz and Prowler now use (S). Offer flat, month-to-month pricing with no meter per API or per framework, because price and lock-in are the most repeated complaints in the whole set. State clearly what data leaves the customer's environment; one Kong Konnect reviewer called sending API metadata to a third-party cloud "a dealbreaker on its own" **[verified, one reviewer]** ([AWS Marketplace reviews](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/reviews/reviews-list/prodview-qwksr2wun4awo)). A check that every route kept its authentication after leaving the retired ingress-nginx controller is a time-limited wedge (M): the project stopped getting security patches on 24 March 2026 **[summary only, official blog]** ([Kubernetes blog](https://www.kubernetes.io/blog/2025/11/11/ingress-nginx-retirement/)), no parity tool was found, but there is also no evidence yet of security regressions from migrations.

## Users complain about price, noise and paywalled logs, not about brokers

The complaint research is strongest on PeerSpot, the one large review site that could be opened. Reddit and Gartner Peer Insights were blocked, and Hacker News searches returned nothing, so the voice of small teams with no security hire is the weakest part of the evidence. No verified complaints were found at all for many vendors, including Orca, Prowler, Aikido, Akto, Oasis, Token Security, Secureframe, Conduktor and Apigee. That is a data gap, not proof that users are happy.

Three lessons follow. First, the loudest pain in cloud security tools is what happens after detection, not detection quality. One reviewer wrote in 2023 that if Wiz "can figure out the remediation workflow" it would be close to a ten, and reviewers in 2026 still describe a backlog. VectaSec's Guided fix and Verify aim at exactly this, and ideas 2 and 5 extend it. Second, buyers of a big platform still feel uncovered: a product leader at a very large company said of Wiz "we still are not getting a complete view", and another reviewer said it "does not allow for tool consolidation". A cheap, narrow tool that fills a named blind spot is a complement to Wiz, not a competitor. Third, and most important for honesty: **nobody in the verified reviews names brokers, gateways or automation tools as the missing part**. The blind spot is described by vendors and one opinion writer, who says security teams have "clear sight into maybe one in 10" connected applications ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4197923/the-saas-blind-spot-why-security-teams-cant-get-inside-their-own-apps.html)). The structural reason is sound: cloud posture tools read the cloud provider's control plane, and a RabbitMQ user list, a Kong admin API or an n8n credential store lives inside the product, outside that view **[inference]**. But buyers do not yet ask for this by name. VectaSec has to show the gap, which is why the free exposure scan and proof mode matter more than another feature list, and why the two entry points where users already feel pain are paywalled logs (idea 1) and compliance evidence for systems Vanta and Drata cannot see (already built as the evidence push; lead with it).

## Six token-theft breaches in fifteen months show where the damage happens

The incident research found three families. The first is the theft of tokens held by an integration vendor, used to read customers' data in bulk. The customer was never "hacked" in the usual sense; a grant they had forgotten was used as designed.

| Incident | When | What went wrong | What would have limited it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salesloft Drift | Aug 2025 | Drift's stored Salesforce tokens stolen; attacker searched support cases for AWS keys and passwords | Narrow scope, IP restriction on the connected app, no secrets in cases |
| Gainsight | Nov 2025 | Tokens between 2 and 8 years old were still valid | Token age report, forced expiry |
| Context.ai to Vercel | Apr 2026 | Tokens of a retired product stolen; an employee had granted "Allow All" | Grant inventory with scope severity |
| Composio | May 2026 | 5,241 API keys potentially exposed at an agent-integration platform | Knowing what is connected through the platform; scoped keys |
| Klue | Jun 2026 | "An old, unused testing credential that was still somehow active" | Stale-credential sweep, IP allow-list |
| ConnectWise Cloud Backup | Oct 2026 | A vendor app secret was used against customers' Microsoft 365 tenants | Inventory of vendor app consents |

Sources: [Google Threat Intelligence](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/data-theft-salesforce-instances-via-salesloft-drift) **[verified]**, [The Register on Gainsight](https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/20/salesforce_gainsight_breach), [The Register on Vercel](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/20/vercel_context_ai_security_incident/), [Composio](https://composio.dev/blog/composio-may-2026-security-incident) **[verified]**, [BleepingComputer on Klue](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/klue-oauth-breach-linked-to-icarus-salesforce-data-theft-attacks), [CSA on ConnectWise](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-connectwise-cloud-backup-m365-credential-2/). Victim counts such as "700+" for Drift appear only in secondary sources and are left out.

The controls that would have helped are all posture facts: the grant exists, its scope is wide, it has no IP restriction, it is old, it is unused. The responders in the Klue case add that "revoking the refresh token, not just resetting a password, is what severs persistent access". This is the strongest argument in the research for a product like VectaSec. It is also a warning about scope. These six incidents happened mostly in Salesforce, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365, which VectaSec does not connect to, and which SSPM vendors were built for. The part VectaSec can own without leaving its lane is the same question asked of the tools it already reads: which automations, MCP servers and gateways hold tokens, how old, how wide, last used when (the access register), and what to revoke on breach day (idea 3).

The second family is unauthenticated remote code execution in internet-facing integration software that stores credentials: file-transfer products (Cleo in December 2024, GoAnywhere in September 2025; [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cleo-patches-exploited-flaw-as-security-firms-detail-malware-pushed-in-attacks/), [Microsoft](https://microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2025/10/06/investigating-active-exploitation-of-cve-2025-10035-goanywhere-managed-file-transfer-vulnerability)), ingress-nginx (March 2025, with about 43% of cloud environments vulnerable according to Wiz), n8n, Langflow and LiteLLM in 2026 ([Wiz](https://wiz.io/blog/ingress-nginx-kubernetes-vulnerabilities), [Rapid7](https://www.rapid7.com/blog/post/etr-ni8mare-n8scape-flaws-multiple-critical-vulnerabilities-affecting-n8n/)). The victims are often small self-hosters, where one engineer runs the tool. The third family is poisoned components: eight fake n8n community packages in January 2026 that received decrypted credentials at runtime ([Endor Labs](https://www.endorlabs.com/learn/n8mare-on-auth-street-supply-chain-attack-targets-n8n-ecosystem)), and a malicious LiteLLM release on PyPI in March 2026 ([Snyk](https://snyk.io/articles/poisoned-security-scanner-backdooring-litellm)). Many of the 2026 CVE numbers and exploited-list dates in the notes come from aggregators and were not checked against the official databases; confirm them before using any in marketing.

On day-to-day operator pain, the picture for automation tools is well supported by incidents, while the picture for brokers leans on vendor blogs. The broker pains are plausible and consistent: ACLs with no owner, shared service accounts, certificate rotation that causes outages. But they come mainly from Conduktor and AutoMQ, who sell the fix, and no forum threads from Kafka or RabbitMQ operators could be retrieved. No verified security incident was found for Kafka itself, service meshes, Apigee, Azure API Management, Workato, MuleSoft or Boomi in the period. The practical reading is that **self-hosted automation and AI tools (n8n, LiteLLM, Langflow, MCP servers) are where small companies are being hurt right now**, and brokers are where VectaSec has depth but less proof of urgent demand. A common thread from the research is worth keeping: each painful job is a join across two systems that neither system does (workflow and credential, person and automation, ACL and real traffic). A product built around those joins is different from a checklist.

## No rival covers the whole layer, and the free baseline is thin

No company was found that positions itself on security posture for the integration layer as a whole. Each family has a specialist, and each specialist leaves the same kind of gap.

| Family | Who is there | What they do | What a buyer still cannot get (in what was checked) | Not checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kafka and streaming | Conduktor, Kpow, Lenses, Confluent, Kong Event Gateway, Gravitee | Consoles with access control and audit; proxies that enforce encryption and masking | A scored assessment of the broker itself; anything for non-Kafka brokers; anything without a proxy in the data path | Aiven, StreamNative, Conduktor Shield docs |
| RabbitMQ, NATS, Redis, cloud queues | Prowler, Trivy, Checkov, Datadog rules | Cloud flags such as encrypted, public, logging | Checks inside the broker (users, permissions, default accounts); Azure and Google messaging in open source | Wiz, Orca, AWS Security Hub, Defender for Cloud, the broker vendors' own advisors |
| API gateways | Apigee Risk Assessment, Defender for APIs, Kong decK, Salt, Akto | One-vendor scoring; traffic-based API security | One rule engine across Kong, AWS, Apigee and Azure; shipped Kong rules | Tyk, 42Crunch, Wallarm, Cequence, Traceable and others |
| Service mesh | Kiali | 72 correctness checks | Judging whether a policy is too wide; score; compliance mapping | Tetrate, Solo, Buoyant, Isovalent |
| Automation tools | Zenity, Nokod, Valence; n8n's own audit | Power Platform, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workato | n8n, Zapier, Make, Boomi, Airflow; many n8n instances in one view | AppOmni, Obsidian, Reco, Grip, Wing, DoControl |
| File transfer | Advisories and exposure counts only | Patch notices | Configuration posture | Tenable and Qualys audit files, Censys, runZero; this conclusion rests on one search |
| MCP and AI gateways | 17 tools including Snyk, Cisco, Docker, Microsoft, IBM, Red Hat, Google | Tool scanning, gateways | MCP joined to the rest of the estate | Cloudflare, Operant, Noma, Pillar |

The open-source baseline can be stated exactly, because the researchers counted the files in the repositories on 23 November 2026. Prowler has 683 AWS checks in total, of which 8 are for managed Kafka, 2 for SQS, 3 for SNS, 4 for EventBridge, 5 for Amazon MQ, 8 for ElastiCache and 2 for Kinesis: **32 checks for all AWS messaging**, every one a cluster-level flag and none looking at users, ACLs or topics. It has no service folder for Azure Service Bus, Event Hubs or Google Pub/Sub, and no provider for Kafka, RabbitMQ, NATS, Redis, Kong or n8n. For comparison it has 23 checks for AWS Bedrock, which shows where maintainers spend their time **[verified]** ([Prowler repository](https://github.com/prowler-cloud/prowler/tree/master/prowler/providers/aws/services)). Trivy has 16 checks across the same AWS services and none for Azure or Google messaging **[verified]** ([trivy-checks](https://github.com/aquasecurity/trivy-checks/tree/main/checks/cloud)). Kong's own linter ships no security rules; the user writes them **[verified]** ([decK docs](https://developer.konghq.com/deck/file/lint/)). Apigee's "multi-gateway" security profiles cover only Apigee products **[verified]** ([Apigee docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/apigee/docs/apihub/manage-security-profiles-multi-gateway)). n8n's built-in audit returns five reports for a single instance **[verified]** ([n8n docs](https://docs.n8n.io/connect/n8n-api/instance-operations/audit)). Against this baseline VectaSec's 440 detectors across 39 connectors are not small. The product is deep where the free stack is empty and has little advantage where the free stack is strong (AWS flags, MCP tool scanning).

Three things are hard for a big platform to copy, and they are where VectaSec should spend its effort **[inference from the notes]**. One is analysis inside the broker, which needs a client for each system's own admin protocol; a cloud API never returns it. Two is credential last-used for systems that do not expose it; Confluent's documented method is to filter audit events by hand, with a seven-day window **[summary only]**. Three is anything that needs all the connectors in one graph: cross-system paths, what-breaks previews, leaver reports. Four things are easy to copy and should not be the headline: cloud flags, MCP scanning, detection of well-known open ports, and compliance mapping alone.

The market has also moved since October. IBM closed its purchase of Confluent on 17 March 2026 and now owns Confluent, IBM MQ and webMethods, so IBM is the party best placed to ship an integration security view for large enterprises ([IBM](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-03-17-ibm-completes-acquisition-of-confluent,-making-real-time-data-the-engine-of-enterprise-ai-and-agents)). Cisco bought Astrix and Cyera bought Oasis for about $1B ([Cisco](https://blogs.cisco.com/news/cisco-announces-intent-to-acquire-astrix-security), [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cyera-acquiring-oasis-security-in-1-billion-deal/)). Two independent non-human-identity vendors have moved up-market, which leaves the small-company end emptier than before. Conduktor is now a direct neighbour on Kafka access hygiene and gives it away.

**The caveats that could change this picture.** The largest untested risk is the big cloud security platforms: Wiz and Orca catalogues were not read, and they may check self-hosted Kafka, RabbitMQ or Redis through workload scanning. The second is the cloud providers' own tools (AWS Security Hub, Microsoft Defender for Cloud, Google Security Command Center), which likely narrow the Azure and Google messaging gap for buyers who use them. The third is Nuclei, a free scanner that almost certainly has templates for exposed brokers, n8n and file-transfer servers, and is the most credible free substitute for the external exposure scan. "No startup found" rests on two web searches; startup directories and 2026 conference lists were not checked. Use the white-space claims in conversations, and re-check them before printing them on the website.

## What not to build: MCP scanning is already free

**Commodity MCP scanning and MCP gateways.** An independent index lists 17 tools, with free ones from Snyk, Cisco, Docker, Microsoft, IBM, Red Hat, Google and the Linux Foundation, and several first-party gateways shipped between June and October 2026 ([Agent Security Index](https://agentsecurityindex.com/category/mcp-security/)). Tool-description scanning, poisoning and "rug pull" detection, and MCP gateways with login and allow-lists are done. VectaSec should keep its MCP connector current as hygiene, not as a headline: map to the CIS MCP benchmark, and update from the 2025-11-25 specification it maps today to the 2026-07-28 specification, which retired sessions, tightened OAuth checks and deprecated the old transport ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)). That is an S-sized update. The only MCP work worth real effort is idea 9, because it uses the rest of the estate.

**Anything in the data path.** Kafka proxies that encrypt and mask (Conduktor, Kong, Gravitee) need client traffic re-routed, and the cost is visible: Conduktor shipped four Gateway security releases in four weeks in October 2026, one fixing identity spoofing **[verified]** ([Conduktor release notes](https://docs.conduktor.io/guide/release-notes.md)). A team of this size should report where protection is missing, and point to those vendors for the remedy.

**Runtime blocking of agents and issuing agent identities.** These are near the peak of hype and owned by Microsoft, Okta, CrowdStrike and well-funded startups. Both need enforcement components that an agentless product does not have.

**An AI that fixes things on its own.** One study of more than 6,000 AI-generated patches found 54% failed to fix the target flaw **[trade press]** ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/black-hat-usa-2026-summary-of-vendor-announcements-part-4/)). A Black Hat 2026 analyst piece called the "autonomous SOC" pitch disproven and said buyers want governed agents, traceable actions and fixes that do not break production ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/09/finding-big-money-ai-smaller-world-security-black-hat-usa-2026/)). If VectaSec later adds write actions, copy the ladder Conduktor published (report, recommend, approve, automate; the agent never has more rights than its creator; every step traced) and stop at "approve" for a long time.

**Traffic-based API security and a full SaaS security product.** The first is an enterprise market with per-API pricing that small teams already reject. The second is tempting because of the six token breaches, but it is another company's product; stage three of idea 3 is the furthest VectaSec should go, and only after checking the SSPM vendors.

**More checks as the main message.** The research gives no support for "more detectors" as a way to feel bigger. The complaint data says the opposite: fewer findings, each proven, each with a fix and a preview of the consequences.

## Conclusion

The feeling of "small" comes from comparing each slice of VectaSec with that slice's specialist: Conduktor for Kafka, Salt for APIs, Zenity for automation, Snyk for MCP. On that comparison any cross-system tool looks thin. The research suggests changing the comparison. The valuable things are the ones a specialist cannot do by design because they need several systems in one graph and a history of scans: time, proof, consequences, people. VectaSec already stores the data for most of them, so the first three ideas together are roughly three to seven engineer-weeks **[inference]**, not a new product.

Two decisions deserve care before building. The first is which pain to sell. The evidence for demand is strongest around self-hosted automation and AI tools and around audit evidence, and weaker for brokers, where the product is deepest; the first paying users are more likely to arrive through n8n, LiteLLM and an auditor's request than through Kafka ACLs. The second is verification of the white space itself. One day spent reading the Wiz, Orca, Nuclei and SSPM coverage lists, and a manual pass through the Reddit communities the researchers could not reach, would either confirm the ranking above or change it, and it is cheaper than any feature on the list.
