# Ship the Access Register, Then Own the Integration Layer

**As of 2026-10-17.** VectaSec already holds the single asset no competitor has — read access to the *acceptance* side of machine credentials inside 39 brokers, gateways, meshes, iPaaS tenants and MCP servers (~440 detectors, `identity.py`, `graph_engine.py`) — but it is missing almost every platform feature that a 2026 buyer treats as assumed, and the one data feed that turns its identity inventory into verdicts. The complete scoped feature set is **116 items across nine groups** (42 table-stakes, 58 differentiators, 16 emerging); of the table-stakes, **~35 are ones VectaSec lacks or only half-has** (SSO, public API, Vanta/Drata push, SOC 2/ISO 27001 catalogue, six-state finding lifecycle with verify-after-fix, CloudFormation onboarding, pricing, a remote MCP server), **~55 are differentiators that no surveyed vendor ships** (derived last-used ledgers for broker/gateway principals, configured-vs-effective authorization probes, cross-product effective-policy and fail-open detection, schema-tag DSPM with CSFLE-gap detection, secrets-in-flow-definition scans across n8n/Boomi/Workato/Airflow, Azure/GCP messaging posture that Prowler and Datadog do not cover, AI-gateway key hygiene), and **~25 are emerging** (CAEP emission, proxy enforcement, agent control planes, A2A card verification, ITDR baselines) that should be designed for but not built in the next two quarters. The sequence that honours the SMB anchor is unchanged from `docs/vectasec-smb-strategy-2026-10.md` for the first eight weeks — last-used telemetry → Access page + auditor register → Guided fix + Verify → SOC 2/ISO + Vanta/Drata push → free external scan → Zapier/automation inventory — and this report adds a concrete weeks 9–26 plan in which the differentiators land in the order that deepens the Access page rather than fragmenting it: runtime-proven access edges, drift with who-changed-it, effective-policy for gateways and meshes, schema-based DSPM, AI-gateway and MFT connectors. The per-system appendix names ~210 specific checks and marks ~45% as already represented by an existing rule family, ~55% as new; the largest new surfaces are service mesh (no `ISTIO.*`/`K8S.*` rule family exists), AI gateways (no family), Kafka ecosystem components (Connect, Schema Registry, kafka-ui), Azure messaging, Zapier/Make, and version-aware ACL-semantics bugs.

**How to read this report.** Evidence labels follow the notes: **[V]** verified from primary docs/standards/CVE records; **[VC]** vendor-claimed (vendor marketing or blog); **[3P]** third-party aggregator, review site or consultant; **[I]** inference by the research team or this writer. Classification: **TS** table-stakes (present at every tier of even the cheapest comparable vendor or demanded by an auditor), **D** differentiator (no surveyed vendor documents it, or only top tiers), **E** emerging (standards or products <12 months old, or needing a sensor/proxy). VectaSec status from the 2026-10-17 code audit: **✔** present, **◐** partial, **✘** absent. Effort is this writer's estimate **[I]**, anchored to the strategy document's sprint sizing: **S** ≤1 engineer-week, **M** 1–2 weeks, **L** 3–5 weeks, **XL** >5 weeks or requires new infrastructure (collector, proxy, sensor). Every effort and dependency grade is an inference; every competitor claim carries the label the note gave it.

## 1. The bar in October 2026: what every RFP assumes and what VectaSec lacks

The "around the detector" layer has polarized. A core set — bidirectional Jira, Slack/Teams, SSO/RBAC, public API, compliance-framework mapping with PDF export, CloudFormation/Terraform read-only onboarding, and trial-to-first-finding in hours — is present on every tier of even the cheapest vendors fetched, while the genuine differentiators moved to a formal triage lifecycle with risk-accepted/false-positive states and verify-after-fix rescans, PR-based remediation with approval gates and independent verification, and a vendor-hosted remote MCP server ([Prowler pricing](https://prowler.com/pricing) [V]; [Semgrep pricing](https://semgrep.dev/pricing) [V]; [Aikido pricing](https://www.aikido.dev/pricing) [V]). Forrester's Q1 2026 CNAPP Wave scores **"Agentic AI and copilots" as one of 12 Current Offering criteria** ([Wiz on Forrester Wave](https://www.wiz.io/blog/forrester-wave-cnapp-2026) [VC]), and Snyk reports Evo at **60% of new deal volume** ([press release](https://cyprusshippingnews.com/2026/09/22/as-ai-risk-scales-and-vulnerabilities-compound-evo-by-snyk-reaches-60-of-new-deal-volume/) [VC]). The best composite lifecycle no single vendor ships in full is Prowler's six states with auto-resolve and regression reopen ([Prowler July 2026](https://prowler.com/blog/prowler-july-2026-newsletter) [V]), Wiz's mandatory reason codes on ignore ([Google SecOps Wiz docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/chronicle/docs/soar/marketplace-integrations/wiz?authuser=0) [V]), Cortex's seven-day time-boxed exception approval ([Cortex docs](https://cortex-docs.paloaltonetworks.com/cortex-cloud-runtime-security/cases-and-issues/customize-cases-and-issues/create-issue-exceptions.md) [V]) and Orca's rescan-on-ticket-close with reopen ([Orca + Jira](https://orca.security/partners/technology/jira-by-atlassian/) [V]).

Against this bar VectaSec has a strong engine and a thin shell: 15 console surfaces, a finding queue with status/assign/suppress and citation-gated AI triage, Slack/Teams/webhook/email/Jira/OCSF notify with routing, viewer/editor/admin RBAC, compliance for PCI/DORA/HIPAA/NIS2/MCP with OSCAL/CSV export — but **no SSO, no public API, no billing, no pricing page, no SOC 2, CI never green on GitHub, on-prem never booted, remediation as 615 free-text strings, and SOC 2 / ISO 27001 absent from `compliance/map.py`** (code audit, `vectasec_current_state.md`). ## 2. Access and non-human identity: the anchor needs a last-used ledger nobody else builds

The NHI market was absorbed in 2026 — Astrix → Cisco (~$400M [3P]), Entro → SailPoint (closed 29 June [V]), Oasis → Cyera (~$1B, closed Sept [V]), Natoma → Snowflake [V], CyberArk → Palo Alto "Idira" [V] — and a KuppingerCole NHI Leadership Compass reportedly named nine leaders, **none of them NHI-native startups** ([AccuroAI](https://www.accuroai.co/blog/nhi-consolidation-identity-acquisitions-ai-agent-security) [3P]). Table-stakes in the buyers' language is now agentless discovery + the tuple identity→credential→grants→owner→consumers + risk score + stale/orphaned flags + one-click revoke/rotate + vault integration + SOC 2 Type II ([Astrix](https://astrix.security/product/) [VC]; [Oasis AWS listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-blsgeikmunnxc) [V]; [OWASP NHI Top 10](https://owasp.github.io/www-project-non-human-identities-top-10/2025) [V]). What none of them do is treat broker, gateway, mesh or iPaaS principals as first-class identities: Entro's catalogue lists Kafka, Confluent, RabbitMQ, Redis, Kong, Zapier, Apigee, Tyk and MQTT credentials only as **secret-detection types with validation, with no last-used, owner or permission enrichment documented** ([Entro supported secrets](https://documentation.sailpoint.com/entro/help/supported-secrets/index.html) [V]; [Entro Confluent key page](https://documentation.sailpoint.com/entro/help/supported-secrets/specific-secrets/confluent-api-key.html) [V]); GitGuardian's source list has no broker/gateway/mesh and **does not store last-used timestamps at all** ([GitGuardian docs](https://docs.gitguardian.com/nhi-governance/discover-your-nhis) [V]); Astrix's named connectors are cloud/SaaS/dev/AI only [VC].

The structural reason is telemetry. Cloud IAM and IdPs expose first-class last-used APIs; integration-layer systems have **no stored per-principal last-used field** and the signal must be derived by continuously consuming audit or connection streams, most of them short-lived or failure-only: Confluent Cloud's audit topic is **retained 7 days** ([Confluent](https://docs.confluent.io/cloud/current/access-management/audit-logging/manage-audit-logs.html) [V]); MSK authorizer logs (launched 6 Aug 2026) capture **denied** requests ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/amazon-msk-kafka-authorizer-logs/) [V]); Redis `ACL LOG` records **only failures** and is absent on Redis Cloud/Software ([Redis](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/acl-log/) [V]); NATS `$SYS.ACCOUNT.*.CONNECT` events are transient ([NATS](https://docs.nats.io/running-a-nats-service/configuration/sys_accounts) [V]); Istio's `source_principal` label is set only under mTLS ([Istio metrics](https://istio.io/latest/docs/reference/config/metrics/) [V]). This is exactly the ledger VectaSec's `runtime_evidence.py` half-builds today from five point-in-time sources, and the strategy document already concedes the anchor "is hollow without it".

## 3. Runtime-proven edges, drift and effective policy are the differentiators no one surveyed ships

Graph leaders converge on risk = path from an exposure through identity edges to a sensitive asset, scored by exploitability × exposure × sensitivity, with "proven" increasingly meaning active probing (Wiz Red Agent [VC]) or observed flows (Illumio [VC]), and choke-point analysis showing **~2% of exposures sit on choke points and prioritising them cuts remediation scope by 99.6%** ([Cyentia on XM Cyber data](https://www.cyentia.com/value-of-choke-points/) [V]). VectaSec's three `ATTACKPATH.*` rules over the label graph are the flagship, but they are static. The open space, verified across the notes, is threefold. First, **runtime-proven access edges**: Otterize's Kafka Watcher proves a client→topic→operation map can be built purely from `kafka.authorizer.logger=DEBUG` ([Otterize](https://docs.otterize.com/reference/network-mapper/kafka-watcher) [V]), Confluent Platform's audit log emits CloudEvents with principal, client IP, `client_id`, listener and granted flag ([Confluent](https://docs.confluent.io/platform/current/security/compliance/audit-logs/audit-logs-concepts.html) [V]), and MSK now streams authorizer decisions to CloudWatch/S3/Firehose ([MSK logging](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/msk/latest/developerguide/msk-logging.html) [V]) — yet GCP Pub/Sub writes **no data-access audit logs for Publish/Pull/Acknowledge at all** ([Pub/Sub audit logging](https://docs.cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/audit-logging) [V]), which a product must disclose rather than paper over. Second, **effective-policy computation**: Linkerd documents no command to view effective policy ([Linkerd](https://linkerd.io/2/features/server-policy/) [V]); `istioctl analyze` has no analyzer for over-broad ALLOW or PERMISSIVE mTLS ([Istio analysis reference](https://istio.io/latest/docs/reference/config/analysis/) [V]); Defender for APIs infers "endpoint is authenticated" from traffic, not config ([Defender API recommendations](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/defender-for-cloud/recommendations-reference-api) [V]); and no surveyed product computes cross-plugin effective policy for Kong/Traefik/Envoy or a cross-mesh authorization graph [gap, I]. Third, the **2025–26 CVE record is dominated by fail-open-on-dependency-failure and authentication-fallback defects** — Istio JWKS-fetch-failure accepting publicly known default keys (CVE-2026-31837 [V]), Envoy `failure_mode_allow` ext_authz bypass (CVE-2024-23324 [V]), LiteLLM OAuth passthrough assigning empty `UserAPIKeyAuth()` (CVE-2026-59822, CISA KEV [V]), LiteLLM salt-key token minting (GHSA-7hp6-4w63-5g45, CVSS 9.9 [V]), Kafka OAUTHBEARER accepting unsigned JWTs (CVE-2026-33557 [V]) — so "flag every config where the auth/TLS dependency failure path is allow" is a cheap, unifying, currently absent rule family [I].

## 4. Gateways, meshes, AI gateways, automation, MFT and agents: the per-category feature set

Authoritative hardening guidance converges on the same ~12 control families across every gateway and mesh — admin-plane exposure, per-route auth with no anonymous fallback, TLS in/out, JWT/JWKS hygiene, rate/size limits, CORS, logging, WAF, secrets-out-of-config, RBAC scoping, version/CVE/EOL, drift — and what differs per product is the config key, the read-only evidence endpoint and the CVE that makes the control non-theoretical ([Kong admin API hardening](https://developer.konghq.com/gateway/secure-the-admin-api/) [V]; [Istio best practices](https://istio.io/latest/docs/ops/best-practices/security/) [V]; [AWS Security Hub API Gateway controls](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/securityhub/latest/userguide/apigateway-controls.html) [V]). Commodity is anything already shipped as a cloud-native control (Security Hub APIGateway.1–.11, Defender's APIM rules) or a linter (istioctl IST0128/0129/0149/0175, Snowcat); unique is cross-product effective policy, fail-open detection, regex-matcher lint, cross-namespace influence graphs and AI-gateway key hygiene [I from gateway note KQ5]. For automation, four blind spots are evidence-backed: SMB automation (Zapier/n8n/Make) holding production credentials while SSPM coverage lists never name them; MFT appliances mass-exploited three years running but not an SSPM/AI-SPM category; enterprise AI connectors with no documented read APIs; and vector DBs default-no-auth with **1,170 internet-accessible Chroma instances** ([BeyondScale citing UpGuard](https://beyondscale.tech/blog/vector-database-security-enterprise-ai) [3P]) and Milvus's default-exposed 9091 management port (CVE-2026-26190, 9.8 — [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/vulnerability-database/cve/cve-2026-26190) [V]). MCP posture itself is commodity by late 2026 — tool-poisoning scans, rug-pull hash pinning and exposed-server discovery are shipped by Snyk Agent Scan ([Snyk Labs](https://labs.snyk.io/resources/detect-tool-poisoning-mcp-server-security/) [VC]) and Cisco DefenseClaw ([site](https://cisco-ai-defense.github.io/defenseclaw/) [VC]) — so MCP stays in the product as a node on the Access page, not a headline.

## 5. Compliance and evidence: the SOC 2 gap is the largest single mismatch

SOC 2 and ISO 27001:2022 are the universal SMB targets, PCI 4.0.1's 52 future-dated requirements became mandatory 31 March/1 April 2025 ([usd.de](https://www.usd.de/en/pci-dss-future-dated-requirements/) [3P]), the HIPAA overhaul is **still an NPRM with final action slipped to July 2027** ([DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/07/hhs-updates-hipaa-rulemaking-timeframes) [3P]), and the EU AI Act's Annex III high-risk deadline moved to **2 December 2027** under Regulation 2026/1744 ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/) [3P]). Yet VectaSec's catalogue maps PCI, DORA, NIS2, HIPAA and MCP — **not SOC 2 or ISO 27001** (`compliance/map.py`). A second structural fact shapes the product: **no CIS Benchmark exists for Kafka, RabbitMQ, Redis, Kong or Istio** ([CIS list](https://www.cisecurity.org/cis-benchmarks) [V]), so checks for those must anchor to CIS Controls v8.1 (4.1, 4.7, **5.5 inventory of service accounts**, 5.4, 6.8), ISO A.8.9, PCI Req 2 — and a vendor can differentiate by publishing its own hardening baselines cross-walked via SCF STRM while stating they are not CIS-certified [I]. Auditors apply four tests to any artefact — **attributable, complete against a bounded population, timely, reliable at source** — and a screenshot after the period "describes the present rather than the audit window" ([idenhq](https://www.idenhq.com/checklists/soc-2-cc6/md) [3P]); the most common NHI exceptions are unowned service accounts, accounts outside the access-review population, and vault-only answers that don't prove inventory completeness ([NHIMG](https://nhimg.org/articles/soc-2-audit-checklists-expose-the-access-review-gap-in-saas-governance/) [3P, vendor]).

## Conclusion

The research changes one assumption in the strategy document and confirms the rest. The assumption it changes is that "last-used" is a connector feature to add; the notes show it is an architectural commitment — a persisted, continuously fed last-seen ledger with precision and confidence per principal, because every integration-layer system either keeps no such field, keeps it for seven days, or records only failures. That ledger is simultaneously the Access page's missing column, the runtime-proven edge in the attack graph, the "allowed but never observed" least-privilege finding, the safe-to-rotate guard on remediation, the alert-history feed auditors now prefer over screenshots, and the baseline any future ITDR would need. Nothing surveyed — not the acquired NHI leaders, not the CSPMs, not Conduktor or Confluent — documents building it for brokers, gateways, meshes or iPaaS. It is the one XL-sized investment that compounds across all nine feature groups, which is why it belongs at week 9 rather than in a 2027 bucket.

The second implication is defensive. The three CVE families of 2025–26 — fail-open on dependency failure, namespace-boundary confusion in Gateway API implementations, and matcher-semantics bugs — are config-derivable and currently un-linted anywhere, as are Azure Service Bus/Event Hubs, GCP Pub/Sub, Kafka Connect/Schema Registry/kafka-ui, AI-gateway virtual keys and MFT token scoping. These are cheap rules (S–M each) against surfaces where Prowler, Datadog and istioctl have nothing, and they are the material for the "fifteen things your cloud scanner and your compliance tool cannot see" sentence the strategy document wants to say. Shipping them after the Access page, not before, keeps the product legible to a buyer with no security team while making the rule catalogue genuinely unmatched rather than merely large.
